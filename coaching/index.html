Individual coaching that fits your week

I build your workouts around your goals, schedule, and equipment. Each week, you send me a short written update about which workouts you finished, what felt difficult, and where you got stuck. I use that check-in to adjust the next week. You can also send a short video of yourself doing an exercise so I can give you feedback on how you move.

When getting started is the hard part

You planned three workouts but completed one. I’d ask what got in the way: session length, your schedule, unfamiliar exercises, or how you felt afterward. If time was the problem, we might start with two shorter sessions and review whether they fit your week.

What I’d change if your squats stopped improving

The situation

You used to do eight squats in a row. Lately you can do five, and you notice you don’t squat as low on the last few.

I’d ask how long you rest between rounds of squats and what else you’ve done for your legs that week. If your legs are still tired from another workout, we might keep the weight the same and do less leg work that week. At your next check-in, I’d look at how many squats you finished and watch a video to see how the last few looked.

How coaching works
  1. We discuss your goals, training experience, schedule, and equipment.
  2. I build your plan and explain how to follow and record it.
  3. You complete your workouts and share your check-ins and any technique clips.
  4. I review your feedback, explain the next steps, and adjust the plan when needed.

Most coaching happens through written or recorded feedback, so you can train on your schedule. Optional calls give us time to talk things through.

What’s the difference between a nutrition coach, a nutritionist, and a dietitian?

Nutrition coach

I help you build eating habits that fit your day. Say you often skip lunch at work. We could look at what gets in the way, pick a lunch you’d realistically bring, and check how it went the next week. My nutrition coaching training covers everyday decisions like these. I don’t prescribe individual meal plans or treat medical conditions.

Nutritionist

A nutritionist gives advice about food and how it affects your health. They might teach a class on choosing foods or work with people individually on what they eat. In the U.S., requirements for using the title vary by state. Some nutritionists are also registered dietitians. If you need help with a health condition, ask about the person’s training and license.

Registered dietitian

A registered dietitian has completed specialized education, trained under supervision, and passed a national exam. You may see RD for registered dietitian or RDN for registered dietitian nutritionist. RD and RDN are equivalent qualifications. A dietitian can help with everyday eating and can build an eating plan around a medical need, such as managing diabetes or kidney disease.
