## 1. Coaching intent
General population and recreational athletes; competitive sport-specific conditioning remains outside automated scope. Train multiple qualities concurrently while shifting emphasis by block. Six qualities: general aerobic fitness, aerobic endurance, sustainable higher-intensity endurance, aerobic power, repeated-effort ability, mixed-movement work capacity. Path A Build Stamina prioritizes sustained work; Path B Improve Work Capacity prioritizes repeatable efforts and eventually aerobic power or controlled mixed movement. Both retain aerobic base. HIIT is a method, not a required destination. No all-out sprints, max tests, or jumping by default. Keep strength/power quality and existing sport workload in mind. Four-week blocks are review windows, not guaranteed progression or mandatory stages.

## 2. Education map
1. Understand conditioning: strength vs sustained and repeated work; benefits and specificity.
2. Energy: ATP, phosphagen/glycolytic/oxidative contributions overlap; oxygen uptake kinetics; colloquial second wind (first minutes sometimes feel easier as adjustment occurs; no guaranteed event; distinct from clinical McArdle second wind); warm-up, fuel.
3. Fatigue/recovery: local vs systemic fatigue, lactate as useful substrate, muscle burn multifactorial, phosphocreatine replenishment, pacing, sleep, heat, hydration, accumulated fatigue; don't push through warning signs.
4. Intensity: plain effort anchors, talk test, optional HR, differing zone systems, VO2max and thresholds, cardiovascular drift, no universal zone-2 formula; optional deeper lesson on moderate/heavy/severe/extreme physiological domains. No equation that claims to locate personal thresholds.
5. Training methods: steady, controlled intervals, tempo, fartlek, HIIT, sprint interval and repeated sprint distinctions, resistance circuit/MetCon. Rest serves the intended adaptation.
6. Build my plan: onboarding, ability, mode, equipment, time, schedule, existing training.
7. My workouts: warm-up, main set, cool-down, accessible timer/log, next workout.
8. Combine with other training: BFit guide links, manage hard days, power first, complementary mode.
9. Progress: comparable output, effort, duration, repeatability, 4-week review, return rules.
10. FAQ: second wind, soreness vs fatigue, sweat, fat burning, EPOC, hitting the wall, why HR rises in heat, why running fitness doesn't fully transfer, cardio and muscle gain. Use question-led lessons, practical examples, optional detail. First-person coaching voice, no forced contrasts, jargon defined when first used, no medical/physiological promises. Explain warning signs before activity.

## 3. Evidence and limits
- WHO activity recommendations https://www.who.int/health-topics/noncommunicable-diseases/physical-activity and CDC https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html/ : build from baseline toward 150–300 moderate or 75–150 vigorous minutes/week (or equivalent), plus strength; this is public-health guidance, not an initial minimum or fixed plan dose. Count actual qualifying activity only, not all session time.
- ACSM/ESSA 2025 intensity terminology https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41085254/ ; CDC talk test https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html : perceived effort and conversational ability practical; do not equate to lab thresholds or fixed HR. Our numeric scale is a BFit coaching anchor, not the Borg CR10 scale.
- 2026 continuous vs HIIT review (115 trials) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41804294/ ; real-world HIIT review https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42506854/ : both routes useful, heterogeneity by population, protocol and outcome.
- 2026 acute near-VO2max review https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42237396/ : intervals >=2 min often accumulate more time near VO2max; acute response is not proof of optimal chronic adaptation, especially novice populations. Longer intervals introduced only after readiness. No guarantee of reaching VO2max.
- Buchheit/Laursen interval programming https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-013-0029-x (part I), https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-013-0066-5 (part II): work duration/intensity, rest, modality and neuromuscular load matter.
- Girard/Bishop repeated-sprint reviews https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21780851/ and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21846163/ : task-specific metabolic and neural determinants. Controlled repeated efforts here are not a validated repeated-sprint program.
- Schumann et al concurrent training https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/ : maximal strength/hypertrophy generally compatible; explosive strength may be attenuated, especially same-session training. Separate priorities when feasible; no guarantee of zero interference.
- Ramos-Campo circuit review https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33924785/ : circuits can improve strength and cardiorespiratory fitness; cannot assume equivalence to endurance or HIIT.
- Oxygen kinetics https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16177607/ ; McArdle distinction https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1344/ ; lactate https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29617642/ ; cardiovascular drift https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22410803/ ; warm-up/cool-down https://www.heart.org/en/healthy-living/exercise-and-physical-activity/fitness-basics/warm-up-cool-down .
- ACSM preparticipation screening https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26473759/ : activity, known disease/symptoms and intended intensity matter; not medical clearance. CDC starting guidance https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-weight-growth/physical-activity/getting-started.html.
Evidence review is targeted, not a systematic search or clinical validation; exact levels, durations, thresholds and review rules below are conservative coach-designed defaults, not research-established cutoffs.

## 4. Eligibility and effort
Adults, stable self-selected mode; ask current activity, recent comfortable minutes in that mode, weekly sessions, recent interval experience, limitations and clinician guidance. Unknown experience starts conservatively. Warning symptoms (chest pressure/pain, fainting, severe or unusual breathlessness, dizziness, new palpitations) block plan and advise evaluation; urgent/current severe symptoms prompt emergency help. Known conditions/medications, recent illness or injury, pregnancy/postpartum and exercise restrictions require appropriate individual advice; no automated vigorous escalation when suitability uncertain. No medical history details required in log. New or worsening movement pain: stop activity, use no pain-provoking replacement; seek evaluation if persistent. Don't wait for second wind. Easy=2–3/10 conversational; steady=4–5/10 full sentences, breathing faster; challenging=6–7/10 shorter phrases; hard=8/10 few words, controlled not all-out. 9–10 not prescribed. Anchors approximate, talk test for appropriate modalities (not swimming mid-length). If breathing/effort disagree, reduce or stop as appropriate. 

## 5. Starting level and weekly prescription
Two primary slots, third optional easy by default. A focused week may count existing relevant exercise rather than adding more. Complementary mode replaces suitable sessions and defaults to no added hard day if current workload unknown. Main work minutes exclude warm-up and cooldown. Each session starts 5 min easy, ends 5 min easy; for hard intervals use 8–10 min warm-up with gradual build, 5 min cooldown; novice short session can use easy transitions in a 10–15 min total visit. Recovery after EACH interval, including last, is counted in timer totals; cooldown additional. No hard interval day until appropriate screening, familiarity and evidence of tolerance. Initial controlled baseline 5–15 min comfortable (or shorter bouts for inactive readers), no max test. Return records same modality/setup; unknown baseline or changed activity starts easier. All tiers may be repeated indefinitely.

| Tier | Entry evidence (coaching gate, not diagnostic) | Path A slot 1 / slot 2 | Path B slot 1 / slot 2 | Optional third |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 establish | Inactive, unknown, or <15 min comfortably | 5–15 / 5–15 min easy; start at 5–10, split into short bouts if needed | Same shared foundation; retain B goal | 5–10 easy if recovered |
| L2 build | Two comparable 15-min easy-to-steady sessions with normal next-day recovery | 15–25 / 15–25 steady | 15–25 steady / 4–6 x 1 min challenging + 2 min easy | 10–20 easy |
| L3 develop | Comfortable 25 min steady on two recent sessions; for B additionally two comparable successful 6 x 1 sessions | 25–35 steady / 20–30 steady OR 2–3 x 5 min challenging + 3 min easy | 25–35 steady / 3–4 x 2 min challenging, 2 min easy; gradually hard only after two successful controlled long-interval sessions | 15–25 easy |
| L4 specialize | Stable activity and two comparable successful L3 specialty sessions, normal recovery; prior relevant training accepted conservatively | 30–45 steady / 2–3 x 8 min challenging + 3 min easy OR 30–45 steady | 25–40 steady / choose aerobic power 3–4 x 3 min hard + 3 min easy OR repeatability 6–8 x 30s challenging/hard + 90s easy OR controlled circuit | 15–30 easy |

Do not force transition from A into B; B L1 users retain chosen goal. A progression from steady to tempo optional and uses same readiness checks. B L4 branches represent different adaptations; short controlled repetitions are not sprint interval training or true RSA; no automated all-out efforts. If established sessions already meet gates, use logged/reported comparable evidence and conservative first session, not an arbitrary month at L1. Longer activity beyond 45 min may be discussed as an individualized next step; no arbitrary cap on real activity. Do not auto-cut existing established training. For 1 day/week, choose alternating primary sessions or one steady session while new; no guarantee of equivalent dose; ask whether another easy activity is feasible.

Illustrative four-week progressions IF readiness met, not calendar promises:
A L2: W1 15+15, W2 15+15, W3 18+15, W4 repeat 18+15/review; optional 10 easy unchanged. B L2: W1 20 steady + 4x1:2; W2 20 + 4x1:2; W3 20 + 5x1:2; W4 repeat 20 + 5x1:2/review. Harder main work = 4,4,5,5 min respectively; interval blocks 12,12,15,15 min; plus 10 min warm/cool at controlled level. Progress one primary variable per slot, prefer only one slot change per week initially. W4 consolidates; reduce 20–30% if needed, not compulsory deload. Progression cadence varies by recovery; no automatic progression for a new week.

## 6. Decision engine (design, not implemented)
Log actual work, actual recovery, completion (full/shortened/skipped), effort, optional output/units/setup, limiting factor (breathing, local muscles, technique, other), symptoms and next-day recovery (normal/extra fatigue/limiting fatigue or pain/not yet known). Compare only same workout family, mode, setup and units. No HR, output or next-day input must be fabricated. Next-day missing = repeat, no escalation. Optional output missing = use self-reported repeatability and target effort, never claim measured improvement; don't upgrade to hard intensity without demonstrated tolerance. Report elapsed work separately from total time. Active recovery not hard work.
- Priority: red flags -> pause/referral; new/worsening pain -> stop mode; excessive fatigue, effort > prescribed cap, unable to recover or visibly declining technique -> reduce or substitute easy next session. Acute concerning symptoms override all. If not back to conversational effort before next interval, extend easy rest; if still not ready after one extension (up to original rest), end challenging work and cool down when appropriate. Log actual completed time.
- Two comparable full sessions at the current prescription in the same slot (or explicitly matched prior training), at prescribed effort or lower, manageable output/quality, both with normal next-day recovery -> eligible to progress one variable. Reset the success count after each progression. Steady add 2–5 min to one session within level ceiling; at <10 min add 1–2. Controlled intervals add one repetition up to cap. Never increase pace/intensity automatically because last interval felt easy; coach cue to adjust to anchor. At cap repeat or enter next gated level, resetting comparison history. L3 hard exposure requires controlled long interval success and separate screening. L4 specialization change creates new baseline. Don't shorten recovery by default.
- One successful session, target effort but challenging, missing recovery or ambiguous quality -> repeat. For fatigue/partial work reduce volume roughly 20–30% or one interval, extend rest as appropriate, never force catch-up. Percent is coaching convention; minimum safe bout and rounding by mode. If output falls >10% from first comparable work bout on a fixed-output mode, flag for repeat/reduction if accompanied by excessive effort or poor repeatability; no automatic physiological inference; first-bout pacing and device noise matter. No output threshold for circuits or variable terrain.
- Missed session resume next unfinished, no doubling; optional sessions never block progression. 7–13 days away repeat last tolerated or easier; 14+ days reduce duration/count ~20–30%, no hard work first return, reassess next-day response. After illness follow appropriate clinical guidance; gaps not a universal detraining rule.
- Weeks are training cycles with dated logs; review total actual steady/interval minutes, matched comparisons, attendance and recovery; no promised improvements. Never silently count external or planned work as completed. Manual log identifies self-report; timer counts actual phases, pause/resume no phantom time. 
- Existing workouts: count relevant duration/intensity once; if existing hard sport/intervals, swap rather than add. Default <=1 dedicated challenging interval session per week; a second optional session is easy only. Avoid hard conditioning near high-quality sprint/jump/lower-body sessions; when combined, perform priority quality first, separate sessions/days when feasible. If sport schedule congested, easy or rest rather than forced sessions. No universal hard-day cap for trained athletes; this is a conservative guide default. Existing strength training retained.

## 7. Modality and circuit rules
Walking (pace/incline, no required running); cycling (cadence/resistance, consistent bike); elliptical (resistance/cadence); rower (technique first, pace/watts); stair climber (speed, safe balance); running (only if already tolerated; Run Strong/5K links, avoid abrupt impact increases); swimming (competent swimmers in safe supervised setting, rest at wall, no breath-holding challenges; use lengths/time and perceived effort, no mid-length talk test; separate session templates and manual tracking, defer aquatic hard sessions until validated). Home marching/low impact step patterns for steady option. Circuits separate progression: e.g. chair squat, standing march, incline push-up, low step-up or supported march, 30s controlled/30s transition-rest x4 x2–3 rounds = 8–12 min main; add rest if technique/breathing requires. Begin with movement familiarity, two rounds and RPE 4–6; progress one round to three only after two controlled sessions/normal recovery. No high-skill, jumping, racing reps, or forced local muscular failure. If local fatigue limits first, swap movement or rest, don't intensify. This is a mixed-movement option, not interchangeable aerobic-power session; report movement and work duration separately. L4 optional circuit can be chosen at lower effort after relevant movement preparation; don't require L4 for simple moderate circuit practice; no auto conversion of circuit minutes to vigorous minutes.
