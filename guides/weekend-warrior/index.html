Weekend Warrior

I built this four-week plan for people who can fit 15–20 minutes at home on workdays and one gym visit on a day off. Use your log to decide when to add a rep or try a heavier weight.

How to use this guide

What you need. At home, clear enough space to lie down. A sturdy counter, backpack, and low step are optional. At the gym, look for dumbbells, a seated row, and a chest press machine.

A rep is one complete movement. A set is a group of reps. One round means doing one set of each listed exercise. If only one session fits this week, choose the full-body gym workout or its home fallback.

  1. Pick your days. Open “Choose your days and time” below. Choose any two days for home workouts and a separate day for the gym. Aim for a day between strength workouts. A walk or gentle mobility fits on the day between them. Allow up to 45 minutes for your first gym visit.
  2. Open your next workout in Week 1. On your home strength day, start with push-ups and split squats. Pick a push-up you can do for six smooth reps and a split squat you can do for six smooth reps on each leg. For your other short workout, walk, ride a bike, or swim if you have pool access. You can also choose my nine-station circuit or a second strength session. The gym card takes you through four lifts.
  3. Write down what you did. A note like “floor push-ups, 8 then 7” tells you where to start next time. Check “I finished this session” when you’re done.

Your two home workouts and gym visit complete the week. You can keep your optional day free or pick easy cardio or a mobility session. If you miss a day, pick up with your next planned session. Use “Training week” below when you’re ready to move on.

Soreness and weekly activity

A new exercise can leave your muscles achy a day or two later. Stop a movement if you feel sharp pain. If pain is severe, gets worse, or lingers, check with a healthcare professional. Cleveland Clinic on soreness

This plan gives you two strength days and a place to start with cardio. The weekly adult target is at least 150 minutes of moderate cardio, 75 minutes of vigorous cardio, or a mix, plus strength work on two days. Brisk walks during the rest of your week can help fill the gap. If you have a chronic condition, ask your clinician what activity suits you. If you’ve been inactive, check before starting vigorous exercise. CDC activity guidance
