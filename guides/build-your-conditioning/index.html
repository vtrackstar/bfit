Before you start

If you have exercise restrictions, a relevant health condition, recent illness or injury, or uncertainty about suitable exercise, seek appropriate professional advice. This guide is general fitness education and training, not clinical assessment, treatment or rehabilitation. It has not been clinically validated and cannot give medical clearance. Stop for new or worsening pain or concerning symptoms. Chest pressure that may indicate a heart attack, fainting, severe breathlessness or other emergency signs require immediate emergency help (911 in the US). Do not wait for symptoms to pass.

Movement instructions

Set up your activity and learn the movements

Practice at an easy pace before using a timer. Choose a familiar activity you can perform safely. If you use a machine for the first time, ask gym staff to show you the controls and how to stop it.

Walking or marching

Choose an even route or clear floor space. Stand comfortably tall, take natural steps, and keep your shoulders relaxed. To adjust the effort, change your pace rather than forcing a longer stride.

Stationary bike

Adjust the seat before mounting so your knee stays slightly bent at the bottom of the pedal stroke. Start at low resistance and pedal smoothly. If unfamiliar with the bike, ask staff to help set it up.

Rowing machine

Set the foot straps and manageable resistance. Push with your legs, then open your hips slightly and pull the handle toward your lower ribs. Return the arms, torso, then knees. Practice before timed work.

Elliptical or stair machine

Use the handles for balance, set a low speed or resistance, and keep your steps smooth. Learn the stop control before starting. If balance or the movement is difficult, choose walking or cycling instead.

Running or swimming

Use an activity you already tolerate. For running-specific practice, see Run Strong. Swim a familiar stroke in a lifeguarded pool with a buddy, rest at the wall, and log afterward.

Four familiar circuit stations

These are practice instructions, not an extra workout. Use the dose in your plan. Move at a controlled pace, and rest or simplify a movement if you cannot maintain control.

1. Chair stand (chair squat)

Place a sturdy chair against a wall. Sit near the front with feet flat. Lean slightly forward, stand up, then sit back down slowly. Use armrests if needed.

Chair stand in Build Your Glutes · Illustrated sit-to-stand instructions (NHS)

2. Standing march

Stand on a clear, level surface. Lift one foot a comfortable height, return it to the floor, then alternate. Keep your steps smooth; use stable support for balance if needed.

Marching illustration and warm-up video (NHS). Use your plan’s duration, not the full warm-up routine.

3. Incline push-up

Place both hands on a stable raised surface that cannot slide or tip. Step back, keep your body straight, bend your elbows to bring your chest toward the surface, and push away. A wall or higher surface is an easier option.

Learn the movement in Your First Push-Up · Countertop push-up video (@clairedefitt, YouTube) · Wall push-up illustration (NHS)

4. Supported low step-up

Use a stable low step and secure support for balance. Put your whole foot on the step, stand up, then step down under control. Alternate the leading leg. If the setup is unavailable or balance is difficult, march instead.

More technique in Foundation Training · Step-ups with light support (Physitrack)
