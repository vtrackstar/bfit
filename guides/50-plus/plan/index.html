03 / First strength session

Practice strength and balance for everyday life

Cardio helps your endurance. To practice lifting, carrying, standing up, and steady movement, add resistance exercises and balance work. Many adults benefit from training all the major muscles on at least two days each week.

150 minWeekly moderate cardio is a general adult goal, not a first-week requirement.
2 daysMuscle-strengthening work each week is recommended for adults.
BalanceEspecially important in guidance for adults 65 and older; practice safely.

Pause before practising these standing movements. If you reported recent falls or unexplained dizziness, use these examples for a conversation with your clinician. Wait for suitable advice before attempting them.

Try your first strength session

Warm up for 3–5 minutes: Walk slowly in place or march while seated, roll your shoulders, and move your ankles. You should feel warmer, not out of breath. If you use a chair, choose one that does not slide or roll.

Then choose three to five of the movements below. One repetition (rep) means doing a movement once. One set is a group of reps before resting. Start with one set of about 6–10 reps per strength exercise, or a short comfortable hold for balance. Rest between exercises.

How hard? Finish each set feeling like you could do about 2–3 more good reps without straining. Move slowly and breathe normally. Use your hands for support when needed.

For shorter sessions, two or three movements are enough to start. If you've had recent falls, repeated falls, or unexplained dizziness, speak to a clinician before trying standing exercises or a new routine.

Legs · Chairs

1. Sit-to-stand

From a sturdy chair, put both feet flat on the floor, lean forward slightly, stand up, then sit down slowly. Use the armrests if needed.

Easier: Use a higher chair or more hand support.

NHS photos: Sit-to-stand (find that heading on the page)
Upper body

2. Wall push-up

Place your palms against a solid wall near chest height. Bend your elbows to bring your chest toward the wall, then push back.

Easier: Stand a little closer to the wall.

Watch the National Institute on Aging demonstration
Calves · Balance support

3. Heel raise

Hold a stable counter, lift both heels to rise onto your toes, then lower slowly.

Easier: Lift just a little, keeping both hands on the counter.

NHS photos: Calf raises (find that heading on the page)
Stairs

4. Low step-up

Hold a secure railing. Step onto a low, stable step, stand tall, and step down slowly. Start with the height you can control.

Easier: Begin with sit-to-stands before attempting a step.

NHS photos: Step-up (near the bottom of the page)
Groceries · Grip

5. Carry a light bag

Hold a light grocery bag at your side and walk a short distance on clear, level ground. Keep your shoulders comfortable and set it down with control.

Easier: Hold the bag while standing with support nearby, or practise with a lighter item.

ACE photos: Farmer’s carry (shown with dumbbells; use a light bag here)
Steadiness

6. Supported balance stance

Stand facing a sturdy counter and hold it with both hands. Gently shift weight from foot to foot without lifting either foot.

Easier: Keep both hands firmly supported and make the shift smaller.

Mersey Care NHS: Weight shift sideways (beginner version)

Each link opens the exercise on the named publisher’s website, not a BFit video. The NHS strength and balance pages contain several exercises; use the heading named in the link to find yours. The ACE carry demonstration uses two dumbbells and is rated intermediate, so begin with the lighter bag described here. For supported balance, use the Mersey Care NHS beginner instructions rather than its harder variations.

The exercises above are a place to start. In your chosen gym or home routine, start with one set of 6–10 reps per exercise and enough rest to keep your technique steady. Add a second set or a little resistance only when your starting version feels comfortable. Choose easier options when you need them.

Balance practice

Start near a sturdy counter with room to move. Try comfortable weight shifts or slow marching while holding on. If you're steady, progress gradually to less hand support or small steps in different directions. Don't practice difficult balance drills alone when falling is a concern.

Mobility and recovery

Gentle shoulder circles, ankle movement, and comfortable hip motion help keep daily movement familiar. Easy days matter. Sleep, joint comfort, medication effects, and the previous day's activity can affect how ready you feel.
