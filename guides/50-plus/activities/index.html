01 / Set your starting point

Start with where you are today

No fitness test required. Choose what sounds most like you right now. You can change your answers later.

This adjusts the general activity guidance. Your exercise experience and how steady you feel also help shape a sensible starting point.

Start with a safety check. Recent or repeated falls, unexplained dizziness, and significant balance problems deserve a conversation with a healthcare professional. A physical therapist can help determine suitable balance exercises. A clinician or physical therapist can help you choose supported movements. Wait for that guidance before trying standing balance or floor drills.

Before you start: Seek urgent medical care for new chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness, or other serious symptoms. If you have a condition affecting exercise or have been told to restrict activity, follow your clinician's guidance. For training, choose stable support, clear floor space, and comfortable footwear.

These are general starting ideas for activity. If you have a condition that affects exercise, use the recommendations from your healthcare professional.

04 / Find your movement

Explore the activities: what to expect and how to start
Cardio · Weight-bearing

Walking

A practical way to build endurance with little equipment. Brisk walking can count toward moderate aerobic activity.

Tips for your first session
Try five to ten comfortable minutes on a familiar, even route. Use a walking aid if prescribed. Add time gradually. Include strength and balance practice alongside walking to work on a wider range of abilities.
Cardio · Low impact

Cycling

Builds heart and lung endurance with relatively low impact. A stationary or recumbent bike can be a more stable choice.

Tips for your first session
Start with easy resistance and check seat height. Outdoor cycling requires confident bike handling and a safe environment. For bone health, add suitable weight-bearing or resistance exercises alongside cycling.
Cardio · Water

Swimming

Builds endurance while water supports your weight. Water walking can be an alternative to lap swimming.

Tips for your first session
Choose a supervised pool and a comfortable depth. If you're new to swimming, start with lessons or water walking. Add suitable weight-bearing or resistance work when bone health is a priority.
Cardio · Social

Zumba, Zumba Gold, and aerobics

Often music-led classes that combine repeated steps and movement patterns. They can improve stamina and coordination.

Tips for your first session
Look for beginner or low-impact classes. Start at your own pace. Choose low-impact steps and comfortable turns.
Sport · Social

Golf

Can add walking, coordination, rotational movement, and outdoor time. The physical workload depends on how you play.

Tips for your first session
Try the range or a short session. Walking the course provides more aerobic activity than using a cart. Warm up before repeated swings.
Sport · Coordination

Pickleball

A racket game played on a smaller court than tennis. You’ll take a few quick steps, stop, and change direction to reach the ball.

Tips for your first session
Try a beginner lesson with a short session. Rapid stops and turns may be too demanding for someone with balance or joint concerns; practice footwork first.
Class · Varies by format

Group fitness

An instructor leads a class, which might include cardio, resistance exercises, or balance practice.

Tips for your first session
Ask what the class involves before joining. Ask how much resistance work the class includes, and choose an intensity that fits you. You can add dedicated strength sessions during the week.
Resistance · Control

Reformer Pilates

Exercises using a moving carriage and spring resistance. It may help with movement control, strength, and flexibility.

Tips for your first session
Book a beginner introduction and tell the instructor about mobility limits. Pair Pilates with gradually progressing strength training when building muscle strength is one of your main goals.
Balance · Mobility

Tai chi and gentle yoga

Typically slower movement, controlled positions, and attention to posture. Some formats support balance and flexibility.

Tips for your first session
Choose a class with chair or supported options. Stay with comfortable movements you can control, especially if you feel unsteady.
