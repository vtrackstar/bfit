Build Your Glutes

I built this eight-week plan for women who want to grow their glutes. Pick two leg days with at least one day between them, like Monday and Thursday. Set aside about 45 minutes for your first visit so you can find the equipment and try your starting weights. I’ll walk you through your first workout and show you when to add a rep or try a heavier weight.

Your first Day A
  1. Look for the hip-thrust machine and a rack you can hold for split squats. If the machine is taken, grab a mat and choose Floor bridge on the first card.
  2. Warm up with a few minutes of easy movement. Try the movement eight times with a light weight. Each time is one rep. If all eight feel easy, add a little. If you shorten the movement to finish, go lighter.
  3. When you find your weight, do your first set. A set is a group of reps. Enter the weight and how many reps you did. For two 15 lb dumbbells, enter 30 lb. For bodyweight or an unloaded machine, enter 0.
  4. Rest about two minutes, or longer if you need it. Do your second set and log it. Check “My reps felt controlled” if you could keep the same movement throughout. Then move to the second card. The third exercise is optional. Rest about a minute between optional sets.

At your next visit for the same lift, look for your target under the demo. If you did 8 and 8 with good control, try one more rep total. That could be 9 and 8.

Why these exercises?

Hip thrusts and bridges train your glutes as you straighten your hips. Squats, split squats and leg presses also work your glutes as you bend and straighten your hips and knees. Hip hinges, such as Romanian deadlifts, train the movement from the hips. The optional abduction and kickback exercises give you another way to work those muscles.

Two leg days give you time to practice the movements and recover between visits. Start with the listed sets and add reps before adding weight. If you already lift, replace similar leg exercises rather than adding both days on top of your current routine.

Eight weeks is a period to practice and track progress. How much your glutes grow varies with training, nutrition, recovery and your starting point.

Soreness, pain, and returning to lifting

Mild muscle soreness can show up a day or two after a new workout. Stop an exercise if you feel sharp pain during a rep. If pain is severe, gets worse, or persists, speak with a healthcare professional. Cleveland Clinic on muscle soreness

If you’re pregnant or returning after childbirth, ask your maternity care team how to adapt these lifts and when to build back up. ACOG on exercise during pregnancy · ACOG on returning after birth
