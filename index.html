Individual coaching that fits your week

I build your workouts around your goals, schedule, and equipment. You record what you complete, and I review your sessions, technique clips, and check-ins to decide what needs adjusting. We also work on practical eating habits you can repeat through a busy week.

When getting started is the hard part

You planned three workouts but completed one. I’d ask what got in the way: session length, your schedule, unfamiliar exercises, or how you felt afterward. If time was the problem, we might start with two shorter sessions and review whether they fit your week.

How I'd handle a training plateau

The situation

You've been completing your squat workouts, but the last set has dropped from eight reps to five for the past few sessions. Your depth also changes when you get tired.

I'd check how hard the sets feel, how long you're resting, and what else you're doing for legs that week. If fatigue looks like the issue, I'd hold the weight steady and reduce some of the other leg work. I'd review your next sessions and a technique clip to see whether your reps and depth improved.

The next adjustment depends on how you respond.

Your training dashboard

You record your sessions, reps, and how training felt. I use those records alongside your check-ins and technique clips to decide what to keep, progress, or adjust.

For example, fewer completed sessions may mean we need shorter workouts. Reps dropping across several sessions gives us a reason to review effort, rest, and workload.

Nutrition tracking is optional. Food records can help us discuss patterns; their totals are estimates, not a diagnosis or prescribed menu.

Nutrition support

I help you turn general nutrition guidance into habits that fit your schedule, food preferences, budget, and training.

  • Understand protein, carbohydrates, fats, hydration, portions, and food labels.
  • Build routines for shopping, preparing food, and eating consistently.
  • Review your food records and check-ins, choose a manageable habit, and follow up on how it worked.

I don't prescribe individualized meal plans, diagnose conditions, or provide medical nutrition therapy. For medical nutrition needs, I refer you to a registered dietitian or appropriate clinician.

How coaching works
  1. We discuss your goals, training experience, schedule, and equipment.
  2. I build your plan and explain how to follow and record it.
  3. You complete your workouts and share your check-ins and any technique clips.
  4. I review your feedback, explain the next steps, and adjust the plan when needed.

Most coaching happens through written or recorded feedback, so you can train on your schedule. Optional calls give us time to talk things through.

Nutrition coach, nutritionist, or registered dietitian?

RoleWhat it means for you
My nutrition coachingGeneral nutrition education, habit coaching, and accountability. I don't prescribe individualized meal plans, diagnose conditions, or provide medical nutrition therapy.
NutritionistA broad title. Qualifications and legal requirements vary by location and credential. Check the person's training and license rather than relying on the title alone.
Registered dietitian (RD or RDN)RD and RDN are equivalent credentials requiring accredited education, supervised practice, and an exam. Dietitians provide nutrition care, including medical nutrition therapy within their professional scope and applicable licensing rules.

If you need a therapeutic diet for a medical condition or support for an eating disorder, I'll refer you to an appropriately qualified professional. With your permission, I can help you put their guidance into practice within my coaching role. Services also depend on the rules where you live.
