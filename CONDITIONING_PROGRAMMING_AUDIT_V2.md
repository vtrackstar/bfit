# Conditioning Training — programming logic audit v2
2026-11-26 | Design review only. Supersedes conflicting v1 thresholds and examples. Fictional tabletop cases, no software or human tests. Full Training Guide; two paths retained. No publication.
 
## A. Deterministic starting rules
1. Screen first: current severe symptoms -> stop and emergency assistance as appropriate; warning symptoms during activity -> pause and medical assessment; clinician restrictions or uncertain vigorous suitability -> no automated vigorous work, appropriate advice. No clearing a medical condition with exercise logs. Screening is not medical clearance. Ask activity history, known relevant condition/restrictions and intended intensity without collecting diagnoses in workout history.
2. Select goal, preferred mode, available days, total visit time (including warm-up/recovery/cooldown), recent activity in that mode and other training days/intensities. Unknown/missing information defaults to easier exposure; uncertain existing demanding workload blocks added challenging work. Strength or another modality is not proof of mode-specific readiness.
3. Same-mode history: preceding 28 days is a BFit recency window, not a biological threshold. Accept explicitly self-reported comparable workouts (date or approximate week, prescription, quality, effort, next-day response) separately from guide logs. No invented entries; self-report is not verified. If inactive or baseline unknown, choose 1–3 min easy bouts with comfortable recovery, up to 3–5 main minutes total initially, or a 5-min easy continuous start if tolerated. Rest as needed; never force continuous work. L1 can progress to 15 min continuous. Show shorter alternative before starting; user may lower dose without penalty.
4. L2 steady eligibility: two comparable recent 15-min continuous easy-to-steady sessions with normal next-day recovery. L2 B interval eligibility additionally requires two same-mode steady sessions at manageable effort (can be the same two) and no restrictions on challenging work. If challenging efforts are unfamiliar, start with 2–3 x 30 s controlled at RPE 5–6, 90 s easy; after two successes progress to 4 x 30 s, then 3 x 1 min with 2 min easy, then 4 x 1 min; no increase to duration and intensity together. At bridge transitions lower count to limit work-time jump. B retains goal while doing base work. If recent comparable 1-min interval experience exists, enter L2 at a conservative matched count.
5. L3 A steady: two recent comparable 25-min steady sessions. Optional tempo has independent gate: two controlled interval bridge sessions at 4 x 1 min (or equivalent recent same-mode evidence), then two sessions at 2 x 3 min challenging + 3 min easy before 2 x 5 min, then 3 x 5 min as tolerated. Never unlock tempo solely because reader can go 25 minutes. L4 A steady: two comparable 35-min steady sessions and normal recovery; tempo branch additionally two successful 3 x 5 min sessions, then 2 x 6 min before 2 x 8; third rep optional after two successes. A can stay steady forever.
6. L3 B: two comparable 25-min steady AND two 6 x 1 min controlled sessions. Enter 3 x 2 min with 2 min easy, same total hard-work time as 6 x 1. Progress to 4 x 2 min after two successes. Hard exposure (RPE 8, never maximal): only after two same-mode challenging 4 x 2 sessions, stable recovery, appropriate screening and explicit opt-in. First hard session 3 x 2 min with 3 min easy, not an increase in total work. L4 aerobic power: two successful comparable 3 x 2 min hard sessions; start 2 x 3 min hard + 3 min easy, then 3 x 3, then 4 x 3 after two successes each. L4 repeated-effort: after L3 controlled gate, start 6 x 30 s at RPE 6–7 with 90 s easy; progress count to 8. Hard option only after separate hard gate and two successful 6 x 30 s controlled sessions; lower count if increasing effort. No all-out sprinting. L4 mixed-movement is a distinct circuit route with its own readiness and no carryover of level or measured output. Ordinary moderate circuits available earlier after technique familiarity. L4 labels mean specialization, not universal superior fitness.
7. Two consecutive comparable successful sessions at exact prescription and slot, both with normal next-day recovery, required per progression; reset after changes/reduction, poor recovery or failed quality. Missing next-day feedback holds progression; late feedback may update recommendation only for next unstarted session. Logs older than 28 days not sufficient to unlock a new intensity/level. Comparable = mode, duration, reps, rest, target effort, relevant setup/units; changing pace within steady target not automatically a new prescription, changing mode or exercise variant does reset comparison. No mandatory weekly progression; no performance promise. Two steady slots can establish entry evidence if prescriptions comparable, but slot-specific progress counters remain independent.
8. Actual planned time cap: calculate warm-up + main work + actual planned recoveries (including final) + cooldown. Normal warm/cool 5+5; hard warm 8–10 and cool 5; short L1 transitions ~2–5 min each with gradual pacing; total visit may be 5–10 min with brief main work, not force extra minutes. If time too short for quality session, use easy short alternative or reschedule; don't cut recovery or warm-up for intervals. If time budget cannot fit a complete bout, no automatic interval prescription. Example: 20 min time budget permits 3 x 1:2 with 5+5 = 19 min; if 4 x 1 prescribed, label reduced variant, not full prescribed progress. Hard 3x2:3 + 8+5 = 28 min. 
9. Steady progression 1 min when <5 main min, 1–2 below 10, otherwise 2–5 min within ceiling; prefer 2–3 by default. Intervals add one rep, only change one primary stress variable at a time (bridge changes may offset count to hold/reduce work). Output/HR optional, never make up readings. A drop >10% flags review only on comparable fixed-setting efforts when accompanied by high effort or poor repeatability; not medical/physiological cutoff. Avoid hard work if unable to recover to conversational effort; extend once, then end demanding work if still not recovered. Any concerning symptoms override.
 
## B. Scheduling and integration
- Focused route: two primary visits, third optional easy; can count existing matching conditioning as a replacement. Complementary route: ask which days already include sport, intervals, lower-body strength, sprint/jump sessions, and user's priority. No automatically imported cross-guide data. Existing demanding game/class/interval session replaces the challenging slot by default, but does not count as a comparable performance test or automatically unlock a higher level. Record external workload only if explicitly logged as self-report; deduplicate by external entry identifier/date/activity and don't credit guide timer for it. A hard game does not necessarily provide equivalent interval stimulus.
- If training availability is one day, L1–L2 default steady; B prepared users alternate steady and controlled interval across available visits (no implied equivalent weekly dose); if external steady already present, a suitable interval may be the sole dedicated visit. Three days: optional third easy only. No catch-up, no automatic doubles.
- Default challenging session away from heavy lower-body training, sprint/jump practice and demanding sport where possible. Prefer at least one intervening easy/rest day from other demanding lower-body sessions for new interval users; if schedule cannot accommodate, switch slot to easy or omit it. This is a conservative coaching rule, not a universal spacing requirement. If both training priorities must occur same day, priority quality first; separate sessions when feasible; never schedule hard conditioning first before priority power. Upper-body strength not automatically equivalent to lower-body hard day; ask how recovered person feels. Back-to-back easy sessions allowed with normal recovery; no hard session if next-day recovery unknown or limiting. An already demanding sport schedule is not a reason to prescribe extra hard exercise.
- Review actual training load and user's goal every four calendar weeks, even if some sessions missed. A missed slot remains next in order but does not create backlog of doubles or force extending a week. Review is not a prerequisite to continue; recommend reassessment when overdue. Optional easy activity does not block progression. 7–13 days gap from comparable mode -> repeat or easier, 14+ -> reduce ~20–30%, no hard first return, reassess; post-illness follow appropriate clinical advice. Recent different-mode activity doesn't remove mode-specific return gate.

## C. Activity-specific implementation
- Walking/home marching: increase duration first, then pace/incline if comfortable; no forced running/jumping. If no safe home floor space, choose other mode. Cycling: resistance and cadence, consistent bike/settings when comparing. Rower: technique before intensity, shorter bouts when technique limits. Elliptical/stairs: stable setup and safe balance, reduce resistance/speed if needed. Running: recent comfortable running history required; otherwise walk or Run Strong/First 5K referral; no treadmill interval sprint defaults. Switching modality resets current comparison and mode-specific ability; can keep separate histories to resume a familiar mode conservatively.
- Swimming: only for water-competent swimmers who can safely complete a chosen length and rest at wall; lifeguarded pool, never alone; no hyperventilation/breath holding, no underwater endurance challenges, no open-water prescription. If not comfortable swimming a length, direct to supervised lessons and land-based alternative. Red Cross https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/water-safety.html ; CDC https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/prevention/index.html . Beginner swim option: 4 x one familiar pool length at easy effort, resting at wall until breathing comfortable (e.g. 30–60 s or longer); can stop earlier. Warm up/cool down with comfortable easy swimming or appropriate safe dry-land preparation; choose familiar distance, no pressure to meet time. Established steady swimmers can repeat comfortable lengths or blocks of 2–5 min, resting at wall when needed, total actual swimming time within selected ability. Progress one length/bout only after two controlled comparable sessions, normal recovery; record pool length and unit (yards/meters), stroke, lengths, actual swim time if known, wall rest separately. No phone interaction mid-swim, no mid-length talk test, no automatic hard swim plan in V1; B goal kept while offering land-based intervals or steady swim. Aquatic technique and safety require specialist review before harder prescriptions. No fake durations for unrecorded swim lengths.
- Circuit: use familiar moves; 4 stations x 30 s work + 30 s rest x 2 rounds = 8 min main (4 work + 4 transition/rest), optionally 3 rounds = 12 min. Begin RPE 4–6, chair squat, standing march, stable incline push-up, low supported step-up or march; allow more rest and lower station duration if needed, end early if technique drops. Progress round or work duration, not both; no forcing reps. Distinguish local fatigue vs breathing, choose lower-demand alternative or rest; don't make circuit faster merely to raise HR. Circuit logged separately, not substituted silently for aerobic power.
