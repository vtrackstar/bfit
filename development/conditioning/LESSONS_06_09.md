## Chapter 6 — Build your conditioning plan
### Where should I start?
I start with what you can comfortably do now. Two people with different goals may need the same first workout.
- **Build Stamina — keep going longer.**
- **Improve Work Capacity — recover between efforts.**
You can change your goal. Neither requires all-out efforts.
### Choose an activity
Choose something you can safely access and repeat: walking, marching at home, cycling, rowing, elliptical or stairs. Running is an option if you already tolerate it; see [Your First 5K](https://vtrackstar.github.io/bfit/milestones/first-5k/) or [Run Strong](https://vtrackstar.github.io/bfit/guides/run-strong/) for running-specific training. Familiar circuits have their own progression.
Swimming is an easy, separate option for people who can safely swim a familiar pool length and rest at the wall. Use a lifeguarded pool, swim with a buddy, never alone. If you cannot swim a length safely, choose supervised lessons or a land activity. No breath-holding, underwater endurance challenges or open-water program. [Red Cross water safety](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/water-safety.html).
### Tell me what you already do
How long can you comfortably perform this particular activity? How often have you done it recently? Have you tried controlled intervals? How did you feel the next day? If you don't know, that's fine; we start conservatively. Recent self-reported workouts can help choose a starting point. No maximum-effort test required. Strength or fitness in another activity does not establish readiness in this one.
Tell me your available days, total visit time (including preparation and recovery), existing strength/cardio/sports and whether more demanding exercise is appropriate. Health screening is separate from fitness history. Follow professional advice and restrictions; the guide cannot provide clearance. See the safety guidance before starting.
### How will this fit your week?
**Make this my conditioning plan:** two main sessions, with an optional easy third visit if recovered.
**Fit it around my current training:** count suitable activity you already do and avoid adding unnecessary work. No automatic sharing between BFit guides.
One day? Start with easy or steady work. A prepared reader may alternate steady and controlled intervals across visits or use a suitable interval when steady activity is already covered elsewhere. One visit is not equivalent to two.
### How we choose your starting point
| Starting point | What we practice |
|---|---|
| Establish | Short, manageable activity; breaks if needed |
| Build | Steady activity or introductory intervals |
| Develop | Longer steady work or controlled efforts |
| Specialize | A goal-specific emphasis after preparation |
You can stay at a level indefinitely. Moderate circuits may suit someone earlier if the movements are familiar. More demanding intervals have separate readiness requirements.
### Example first visit
If you can comfortably cycle for five minutes, start with five easy minutes warming up, five easy minutes cycling and five easy minutes cooling down (15 minutes total). If five continuous minutes is too much, use shorter easy bouts: for example, one minute of easy walking, a comfortable rest, another minute if ready, with a gradual start and finish. Stop earlier if needed. No need to force five minutes or an arbitrary rest duration. Your goal can remain improving work capacity while you build a base.

## Chapter 7 — Your workouts
### What should I do first?
Set up your equipment safely and begin at an easy pace. Follow your plan's work and recovery periods; ease down at the end if appropriate. Record what you actually did. If something feels unusually wrong, stop rather than finishing a timer. See safety guidance.
### Example steady workout (prepared reader)
| Part | Time | Action |
|---|---:|---|
| Warm-up | 5 min | Walk slowly or pedal with light resistance |
| Main | 15 min | Steady effort, 4–5/10; full sentences possible |
| Cooldown | 5 min | Ease the pace |
Total 25 minutes. If you cannot yet tolerate 15 minutes, begin with the shorter plan suggested for you.
### Example introductory intervals (prepared reader)
| Part | Time | Action |
|---|---:|---|
| Warm-up | 5 min | Pedal easily |
| Work | 30 sec | Increase to controlled effort 5–6/10 |
| Recover | 90 sec | Pedal easily or rest safely |
| Repeat | 2 more pairs | Three work/recovery pairs total |
| Cooldown | 5 min | Ease the pace |
Main block 6 minutes: 90 seconds work, 270 seconds recovery. Total 16 minutes. If breathing does not settle, extend recovery once if appropriate; if still not ready, end the demanding portion and cool down if safe. Do not continue through warning symptoms.
### Only have 20 minutes?
A four-interval session (one minute work, two minutes easy) takes 22 minutes with preparation and cooldown. Three intervals take 19 minutes. Choose a time-adjusted version or an easier alternative. Do not remove recovery to squeeze in another interval. Short on time is different from unable to finish because of fatigue.
### What should I record?
Record actual work, effort, movement quality, extra rest and why you stopped early if applicable. Optional output: distance, watts or pedal revolutions per minute (rpm) with the same equipment/settings. A timer measures time, not whether you physically exercised. Confirm actual exercise; leave unknown measurements blank. Manual records are self-reported.
Check recovery the following day: normal for you, more tired than usual, or fatigue limiting ordinary activity. Use the safety route for pain or concerning symptoms. Do not record next-day recovery before the next day.
Swimming: record actual familiar lengths, pool length and units, time and wall rest if known, after leaving the water. No phone interaction in the pool.
### If you need to stop or miss a visit
Pause when necessary, finish early or skip if you did not start. Record only what happened. Resume training without doubling demanding sessions. Phone browsers may suspend timers when locked/backgrounded; confirm uncertain time rather than automatically counting it. Use manual recording if a phone is unsafe or impractical. These controls must work before they appear in the published guide.

## Chapter 8 — Combine conditioning with your other training
### Do I need to add more cardio?
Maybe not. Games, classes and existing cardio contribute to your workload. A hard game is not the same as a controlled interval test. Count activity once and do not assume every minute qualifies as moderate/vigorous aerobic exercise. Decide which quality matters most right now.
| Current training | How I would fit conditioning |
|---|---|
| [Foundation](https://vtrackstar.github.io/bfit/guides/foundation/) | Keep useful lifting; start with manageable aerobic work |
| [Athleticism](https://vtrackstar.github.io/bfit/guides/athleticism/) | Protect quality sprinting, jumping and skill |
| Run Strong / First 5K | Count relevant running; add another mode only if useful |
| [Weekend Warrior](https://vtrackstar.github.io/bfit/guides/weekend-warrior/) | Replace suitable cardio slots; keep the time budget |
| [Build Your Glutes](https://vtrackstar.github.io/bfit/guides/build-your-glutes/) | Manage leg fatigue around strength sessions |
| [Keep Moving 50+](https://vtrackstar.github.io/bfit/guides/50-plus/) | Choose appropriate comfortable activity; maintain balance and strength work |
| [Gamer Fit](https://vtrackstar.github.io/bfit/guides/gamer-fit/) | Include short movement breaks without treating them automatically as hard conditioning |
| Sport / group fitness | Count demanding activity; easy work or rest may fit better than more HIIT |
Aerobic and strength training can generally coexist; explosive strength may be more sensitive to combined demanding sessions. If priorities share a day, do the priority quality work while fresh, separate demanding sessions where practical, and adjust if recovery suffers. No guarantee of zero interference and no universal mandatory rest-hours rule. [Concurrent training meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/).
### Example weeks, not universal prescriptions
| Day | Strength priority | Conditioning priority (prepared reader) | Basketball week |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Upper + easy bike if recovered | Steady bike | Easy walk |
| Tue | Lower strength | Strength | Rest |
| Wed | Rest | Rest | Strength |
| Thu | Upper + easy bike if recovered | Controlled intervals | Easy activity |
| Fri | Lower strength | Rest | Rest |
| Sat | Rest | Strength if recovered | Game |
| Sun | Easy or rest | Optional easy activity | Rest |
The game already represents demanding work; no automatic extra HIIT. If quality declines, modify the schedule. Beginners should leave easy/rest time between demanding lower-body days when practical. If no demanding slot fits, omit or replace it with suitable easy work.
### If the week changes
Change future days without rewriting history. Back-to-back easy sessions can be appropriate if recovered. No catch-up doubles. After a week away, repeat a suitable previous dose or go easier. After about two weeks away from that activity, reduce the return workload and avoid hard efforts initially. These are conservative coaching defaults, not exact detraining thresholds. After illness/injury, follow individual guidance. Do not override safety concerns to stay on schedule.

## Chapter 9 — How do I know if I'm improving?
### Look for useful changes
| Goal | What to compare |
|---|---|
| Stamina | Comfortable duration, effort at a familiar setting |
| Repeated efforts | Similar output across bouts, completed work and recovery |
| Circuits | Controlled movement and manageable fatigue |
| Swimming | Familiar lengths and effort, actual time if known |
Compare like with like: same activity, settings, units, effort and relevant conditions. Different machines and terrain can make output incomparable. One workout cannot prove an adaptation.
### Example: sustaining activity
Fictional cycling example, same resistance and comparable effort: 15 minutes at 4/10 earlier, 18 minutes at 4/10 recently. That is encouraging evidence of longer sustained work, not a measured VO₂ max improvement.
### Example: repeating efforts
Fictional four one-minute efforts, two minutes easy, same stationary bike and resistance.
| Effort | Earlier rpm | Recent rpm |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 80 | 80 |
| 2 | 77 | 79 |
| 3 | 73 | 78 |
| 4 | 70 | 78 |
First-to-last decline: 12.5% earlier, 2.5% recently. If effort and recovery were similar, recent execution was more consistent. Familiarization, pacing and measurement variation can also explain changes. Repeat before drawing conclusions. RPM is pedal revolutions per minute; watts measure power, not the same thing. Use an accessible chart only with comparable records.
### Why hasn't my workout changed?
I want you to repeat a workload before increasing it. Our default is two comparable successful sessions at the current prescription with controlled movement, intended effort and normal next-day recovery. If the prescription changes, the comparison starts again. This is a coaching rule, not a universal biological cutoff.
| What happened | Next decision |
|---|---|
| Two successful comparable visits | Consider a small progression if suitable |
| One success or missing recovery | Repeat or hold |
| Ended early due to time | Return to planned dose if appropriate |
| Excessive effort or poor quality | Reduce, adjust or rest |
| Changed activity | Establish new baseline |
| Warning symptoms or new pain | Stop and follow safety guidance |
No default shortening of rests or automatic escalation because another week passed.
### Four-week review
Review actual completed, shortened and skipped visits; known main work, recovery and total time separately; optional and outside activities separately; missing measurements and recovery. Prior training informs starting point but is not guide attendance. Swimming lengths can be reported without inventing time.
Illustrative review: two 18-minute visits at the same setting felt manageable, with normal next-day recovery. A small steady-duration increase may be suitable after schedule and readiness checks. If comparable information is missing, say so. A complete attendance denominator requires scheduled visit records; do not invent it. Choose whether to maintain, adjust or change your goal.
### Keep your records
Local browser records are device-specific; clearing browser data or changing devices can remove access. The planned backup/restore option must be tested before release. No accounts, automatic syncing or transfer between guides. A record is not proof exercise occurred. Body weight, photos, calorie estimates and a watch are not required.
