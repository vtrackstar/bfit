## Chapter 10 — Common questions
### What your body is doing
**Do I need to catch a second wind?** No. Warming up and finding a sustainable pace may help you feel more comfortable. Never push through concerning symptoms waiting for it. See Chapter 2.

**Does lactate make me sore the next day?** Lactate is not the cause of delayed-onset muscle soreness. The burning feeling during exercise and soreness afterward are different; neither proves a workout was effective. [Lactate and soreness review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13559195/). See Chapter 3.

**What does hitting the wall mean?** A major drop in sustainable performance during prolonged endurance exercise, commonly discussed in marathon running. Reduced carbohydrate availability, pacing, heat and other fatigue factors can contribute; this isn't ordinary tiredness after a few minutes. Unexpected severe symptoms require stopping and appropriate help. [Marathon-specific review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42647332/). See Chapters 2–3 and Run Strong for running-specific applications.

**Does sweating more mean I got fitter?** Sweat helps cool your body as it evaporates. Heat, clothing and individual differences affect it. Do not deliberately overheat or restrict fluids to sweat more. [Heat stress review](https://doi.org/10.1152/physrev.00038.2020).

### Intensity and energy use
**Is Zone 2 best for burning fat?** Fat may provide a larger *proportion* of energy at easier intensities. The actual amount used per minute can behave differently, and neither measurement guarantees body-fat loss over time. Choose activity you can sustain and recover from. [Fuel use review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12375902/); [Body composition comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34822354/); [umbrella review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39003682/). See Chapter 4.

**Is the afterburn effect real?** Yes: oxygen consumption can remain above resting levels after exercise, called excess post-exercise oxygen consumption (EPOC). The extra expenditure varies; harder workouts can increase it, but it is not a guarantee of fat loss or a reason to maximize fatigue. [EPOC systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32656951/).

**Do I need a heart-rate monitor?** No. Use effort, breathing and movement quality. A watch adds information but cannot override symptoms or determine all training demands. See Chapter 4.

**Is HIIT better than steady activity?** Both can improve aerobic fitness; the right method depends on your goal and readiness. You don't need HIIT to progress. See Chapter 5.

**Is MetCon the same as HIIT?** Not necessarily. A circuit may challenge your muscles before your breathing becomes very demanding. Choose the method based on what you want to improve. See Chapter 5.

### Making conditioning fit your life
**Will cardio ruin my muscle gains?** Aerobic and resistance training can generally coexist; explosive strength may be more sensitive to combined demanding sessions. Manage workload and priorities. [Concurrent training meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/). See Chapter 8.

**Can I improve without running?** Yes. Walking, cycling, swimming and other suitable activities can develop aerobic fitness. For running performance, running-specific practice matters. See Chapter 6.

**Why am I fit at cycling but struggle with another activity?** Some adaptations transfer, but movements, muscle demands, coordination and impact differ. Establish a new baseline. [Running/cycling cross-training review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42267259/). See Chapters 1 and 6.

**I already play sports or attend classes. Do I need another interval day?** Not necessarily. Count existing demanding work; easy activity or rest may be more useful. See Chapter 8.

**Why hasn't my plan increased?** Repeating a useful workout helps establish readiness. Missing recovery, changed activities or excessive fatigue can delay progression. See Chapter 9.

**What if I'm too tired to finish?** Slow down, recover or stop the demanding work; record what happened. Don't force a timer or double up. New pain or concerning symptoms require stopping and following safety guidance. See Chapters 3 and 7.

**Can I change my goal or activity?** Yes. Keep your past records and establish a suitable baseline for the new activity. See Chapter 6.

### Optional glossary
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ATP | Molecule muscles use to power contraction |
| Aerobic | Processes that use oxygen to help supply energy |
| Anaerobic | Processes that do not directly require oxygen |
| Aerobic power | Rate of aerobic energy supply; VO₂ max reflects maximal oxygen uptake |
| Anaerobic capacity | Amount of energy available from nonoxidative pathways during demanding work |
| RPE | Rating of perceived exertion: how hard the effort feels |
| Interval | Planned period of work or recovery; a session alternates them |
| Tempo | Broad label for sustained purposeful effort |
| RPM | Revolutions per minute |
| Watts | Units of power (rate of work) |
| Recovery | Rest or easier activity between efforts; also adaptation/recovery between sessions |
| Work capacity | Ability to handle and repeat physical work, as used in this guide |
| Training density | In our comparisons, work time relative to the total main block |
| VO₂ max | Maximum rate of oxygen uptake and use during intense exercise |
