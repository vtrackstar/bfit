# Keep Moving 50+: voice, research, and visual-integrity review
**Reviewed:** November 19, 2026  
**Scope:** Four-page Keep Moving redesign, including the intro, activity picker, strength builder, weekly review, sourced diagrams, and shared navigation.

## The voice we were aiming for
The BFit hub speaks in the first person, uses ordinary situations as examples, and tells readers what to do next. Keep Moving should sound like Bryan explaining training to someone who is interested in starting, not a narrator selling a dramatic transformation.

## Edits and checks
- **Start with a real goal.** The opening asks what the reader would like their body to keep doing. Groceries, stairs, a walk, and recreation are specific examples. It does not open with alarmist age-related decline.
- **Keep actions short.** Four clearly labeled pages, a direct route to the plan, and instructions for the first workout. No mandatory quiz or long educational prerequisite.
- **Use a consistent speaker.** First-person coaching statements in the introduction and footer, rather than introducing a fictional BFit spokesperson or using machine-like corporate copy.
- **Use U.S. English consistently.** “Practice” rather than alternating with “practise”; “sets,” “reps,” and “balance” explained when used for a first workout.
- **Avoid filler patterns.** No “unlock your potential,” “game-changer,” “in today's fast-paced world,” rhetorical fake anecdotes, or stacked motivational slogans. No repeated em-dash qualifications.
- **Keep science claims appropriately narrow.** A 2024 review summarized resistance-training studies in older adults; the LIFTMOR study involved screened postmenopausal women in supervised high-intensity exercise; the Cochrane fall-prevention review covered organized exercise programs among community-dwelling older adults. The page does not suggest these studies prove a specific BFit routine will achieve the same results.
- **Be truthful about visuals.** Both diagrams are original simplified SVG illustrations made for BFit, not medical scans, real patient samples, training photos, or before-and-after study results. Their captions say so. We have not generated fake MRI comparisons, imaginary clients, or a synthetic presenter.
- **Credit demonstrations.** Exercise links identify the NHS, National Institute on Aging, ACE, and Mersey Care NHS as their source. Third-party media is not presented as BFit-produced.
- **Make supporting claims checkable.** Source links appear directly by the finding, with a full further-reading section. Research explanations are expandable so readers who just want a plan can skip them.
- **No coercive coaching funnel.** The guide works without registering or paying. The coaching link points to the paused-intake information page.
- **Protect private answers.** Saved preferences and weekly reviews stay in the browser unless the reader exports a backup. The wording does not pretend the site has accounts or server-side workout logging.

## Evidence used in the intro
1. CDC: [Activity guidelines for adults aged 65+](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/older-adults.html) and [adults 18–64](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html).
2. National Institute on Aging: [Health benefits of exercise and activity](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/exercise-and-physical-activity/health-benefits-exercise-and-physical-activity).
3. Resistance-training volume review (151 randomized trials, 2024): [PubMed 39405023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39405023/).
4. LIFTMOR randomized bone-health trial (supervised, screened postmenopausal women with low bone mass): [PubMed 28975661](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28975661/).
5. Cochrane review of exercise for preventing falls in community-dwelling older adults: [CD012424](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD012424_exercise-preventing-falls-older-people-living-community).
6. NIAMS: [Exercise for your bone health](https://www.niams.nih.gov/health-topics/exercise-your-bone-health).

## What the audit can and cannot establish
Readable copy, source transparency, specific coaching decisions, and accurate image labels make the guide easier to trust. They do not prove how or by whom every line was originally drafted, and the site makes no misleading human-only authorship claim. The objective is a useful, accountable coaching resource, not to conceal use of tools.

Automated CI checks navigation, responsive layouts, key saved-state paths, accessibility rules, exercise references, and 108 schedule combinations. **Still needed before a wide promotional push:** independent-reader observation, a real iPhone/Android trial (particularly Print/Save as PDF), a manual screen-reader walk-through, and editorial verification of the movements shown in every linked third-party demonstration.
